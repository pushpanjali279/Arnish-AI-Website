Hospital Pre-Authorization Automation for Cashless Claims in India

Hospital pre-authorization automation is software that prepares, sends and tracks cashless requests to insurers and TPAs, filling forms from the hospital information system so the insurance desk retypes less and answers fewer queries. Arnish AI, a software company based in Shirpur, Maharashtra, builds these tools for hospitals in India.

By the Arnish AI teamPublished 3 November 20269 min read

What is hospital pre-authorization automation?

It is software that handles the clerical side of a cashless claim. It gathers the documents each insurer or TPA wants, fills the request from data the hospital already holds, sends it, and tracks every reply until the patient goes home.

The approval decision stays with the insurer, and the clinical content stays with the treating doctor. Software takes over work that needs care but not judgement: finding each payer's form, attaching reports, copying patient details into several portals and checking for replies.

For a TPA desk manager, a useful target is fewer files sitting in query status and fewer families waiting at the billing counter on discharge day.

How does a cashless claim move from admission to discharge?

A cashless claim passes four checkpoints: the pre-authorization request, queries from the payer, enhancement requests if costs rise, and final authorization at discharge. Each one can stall the file.

1. Pre-authorization request

When a cashless patient is admitted, or before a planned admission, the desk sends the insurer or TPA a request with policy details, the doctor's provisional diagnosis and plan, the expected stay and a cost estimate. Each payer asks for different supporting documents through a different portal or email address.

2. Queries

The payer approves an initial amount, denies the request, or raises a query asking for reports, past history, a clearer diagnosis or a reason for the procedure. Each query adds a round trip, often waiting on a doctor who is on rounds.

3. Enhancement

If the stay runs longer or the treatment changes, the desk asks for an enhancement with updated notes and a revised estimate. A long admission can need more than one.

4. Final bill and discharge approval

On discharge day, the hospital sends the final bill and discharge summary for final authorization. The insurer approves its share, and the patient pays for non-payable items, any co-payment and anything above policy limits. Delay is most visible here, with the family waiting to leave.

How should the insurance desk plan its daily queue?

Sort the day's cashless files by what each patient is waiting for, so the file holding up a patient goes first. Discharges due today lead, then planned admissions without approval, then old queries, then running bills near their approved amount.

Working the inbox in arrival order can leave a family waiting at billing while the desk handles new requests. A workable morning order is:

  1. Discharges planned for today, starting with those the doctor has signed off.
  2. Planned admissions for today and tomorrow without an initial approval.
  3. Queries older than the desk's target reply time, grouped by the doctor who must answer.
  4. Patients whose running bill is near the approved amount.

Emergency admissions interrupt this list, so it must be easy to re-sort. Software can rebuild it from the HIS and the query tracker as replies arrive, with each row showing the patient, payer, next action and its owner. The same list works as the shift handover note.

Where do pre-authorization delays and queries come from?

Delays usually trace back to missing documents, unclear clinical details, tariff mismatches and the work of retyping data into several insurer and TPA portals.

Missing or wrong documents

Each payer has its own document list for each kind of case, and accident cases often bring extra document requests from the payer. When these lists live in staff memory, a new joiner or night shift misses one, and the payer queries something the hospital already had.

Unclear diagnosis or treatment details

A request that says "abdominal pain, admitted for evaluation" gives the reviewer little to approve. Reviewers look for a provisional diagnosis, findings, the planned treatment and why inpatient care is needed. Illegible notes, missing diagnosis codes, or a procedure that does not match the stated diagnosis all lead to queries.

Tariff and package mismatches

Hospitals agree package rates, room categories and tariffs with each insurer and TPA. If an estimate uses the general tariff instead of the agreed one, or books a room above the policy's room rent eligibility, the approved amount comes back lower or a query follows.

Retyping into multiple portals

Staff type the same name, policy number, diagnosis and estimate into the hospital information system (HIS), then into each payer's portal, then check email and every portal for replies. Each retyping risks a wrong digit, and each portal nobody checked is a query left unanswered.

What can automation do at each stage of a cashless claim?

At each checkpoint, software can check completeness before sending, fill forms from existing data and watch for replies, so problems show up while the desk can still fix them.

Document checklists by payer and case type

The hospital records once which documents each payer wants for each kind of admission. The software shows that list, marks what is already in the HIS and holds the request until missing items arrive or staff record a reason.

Pre-filled requests from the HIS

Demographics, policy details, admission date, treating doctor and estimate already sit in the HIS. Pulling them into the request removes most retyping, and one record can fill several portals. Staff review the form instead of building it.

One tracker for pending queries

One list shows every open file, its status, the last reply and who owns the next action, whether replies came by portal or email. The desk manager can see which queries are getting old and which doctor is holding them.

Where AI helps, and where plain rules do the job

Most of the above is rules and integration. AI-powered hospital automation software helps in narrower places: reading scanned investigation reports, suggesting diagnosis codes for a coder to confirm, and summarising a long case sheet so the doctor can answer a query faster. How should the desk handle enhancement requests during a stay?

Raise an enhancement before the running bill crosses the approved amount, with updated clinical notes and a revised estimate that explain what changed. Late or unexplained enhancements lead to queries and to disputes over the bill at discharge.

Common triggers are an extended stay, a move to the ICU, an added procedure, or high-cost drugs or implants. If the HIS records an ICU transfer or a new procedure order, the software can flag the file the same hour instead of waiting for the bill to catch up.

A clear request reads like a short update from the treating doctor: the change in condition, the revised plan and length of stay, the new cost, and the reports behind it, such as a repeat scan. A request that only shows a bigger number invites a query.

What should the desk track at discharge?

Track the time from the doctor's discharge sign-off to the file being sent, the time from sending to final approval, and every deduction with its stated reason. Together these show whether discharge delays start inside the hospital or with the payer.

The two parts need different fixes. A slow hospital side usually means an unfinished discharge summary, unposted pharmacy returns or an unchecked bill. A slow payer side calls for figures by payer and case type to raise with that payer. Before the file goes out, check that:

  • The discharge summary is signed and its diagnosis matches the pre-authorization or explains the change.
  • Bill lines use the agreed tariff or package, and the room category matches the approval.
  • Every enhancement approval is attached.
  • Non-payable items are listed separately, so staff can explain the patient's share early.

Deductions that repeat, such as a consumable a payer does not cover under a package, can then be explained to the family at admission instead of at the billing counter.

Where does NHCX fit into pre-authorization automation?

The National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX), run by the National Health Authority, gives hospitals and payers one standard way to exchange cashless messages, so a connected hospital can send requests in one format instead of using a portal per payer.

NHCX uses HL7 FHIR profiles published in the FHIR Implementation Guide for ABDM. Its profiles cover coverage eligibility checks, pre-authorization, claims and claim responses. A pre-authorization and a final claim use the same Claim structure, with a field that marks which one it is.

Ask each of your empanelled insurers and TPAs whether it accepts requests through the exchange. The data a hospital must produce is the same either way. If your HIS data can be shaped into a clean FHIR claim, the same record can go through NHCX where accepted and fill the remaining portals.

What should a hospital not automate?

Clinical wording, admissibility calls and any decision that commits a patient to paying money should stay with people. Software can prepare and check these items but should not decide them.

  • Software should not write a clinical justification the doctor has not written. It can pull in the doctor's notes and flag empty fields.
  • Suggested diagnosis and procedure codes need review by a trained coder or the treating doctor before they go out.
  • Whether to accept a partial approval, contest a denial or ask the patient to pay the difference is for the desk manager.
  • Keep a human check before sending, at least for the first months. A wrong attachment shows one patient's records to another patient's payer.
  • Conversations with families about deductions and out-of-pocket amounts belong to staff.

Data handling needs the same care. Limit which staff and systems can see full patient records, encrypt files in storage and in transit, and keep an audit log of who sent which file to which payer. Review access when staff change roles or leave.

How should a hospital start with pre-authorization automation?

Measure first, then automate the step that causes the most queries. For many desks that means document checklists and a query tracker before any deeper integration.

Track every cashless file for a few weeks: payer, case type, number of queries, the reason for each query and the time from discharge sign-off to final approval. The pattern usually points to a few payers and case types.

Frequently asked questions

How do we measure whether pre-authorization automation is working?

Compare the same measures before and after: queries per file, the share of files approved without a query, time from discharge sign-off to file sent, time from sending to final approval, and deductions per file. Track them by payer and case type, because an average across all payers can hide where the change happened.

Can pre-authorization automation work with our existing hospital information system?

In most cases, yes. The software reads patient, policy and billing data from the HIS through an integration, a database view or a scheduled export, depending on what the HIS supports. Replacing the HIS is not needed to automate the insurance desk.

Does automation replace TPA desk staff?

No. It removes retyping, checklist work and portal checking, so desk staff spend their time on queries that need a doctor's input and on conversations with patients. Decisions on clinical wording, partial approvals and patient payments stay with people.

Can the same software handle enhancement requests during a stay?

Yes. It can compare the running bill with the approved amount, flag events such as an ICU transfer or an added procedure, and pre-fill the enhancement with the updated notes and revised estimate. The treating doctor still writes or confirms the clinical update before the desk sends it.

What should a hospital automate first?

Start with insurer-specific document checklists and a single tracker for open queries. Both need little integration, and they address common reasons a cashless file stalls. Pre-filled forms and discharge timers can follow once the data from your HIS is clean.
