TPA Software in India: Features to Check Before You Buy or Build

TPA software in India has to run member enrolment, the hospital network, cashless pre-authorization, reimbursement claims and reporting for several insurers at once, with a separate rule set and audit trail for each one. Arnish AI, a software company based in Shirpur, Maharashtra, builds claim and TPA software and wrote this checklist for buyers.

By the Arnish AI teamPublished 3 November 202610 min read

What does TPA software need to do in India?

It has to carry a health policy from the day a member is enrolled to the day the insurer receives a processed claim and a report. A Third-Party Administrator sits between insurers, hospitals and policyholders, so the software serves all three groups and keeps their records apart.

Much of the work is preparation. The platform collects documents, checks them against the policy and drafts a recommendation for the insurer client. Design the software so the recommendation and any approval are separate, recorded steps, with the person or rule behind each one visible to whoever reviews the claim later.

How should it handle member enrolment and e-cards?

Enrolment should accept member data in the formats insurers and corporate HR teams send it, validate it, and issue an e-card the member can show at a hospital. Many errors that surface later at the cashless desk start here, as a wrong date of birth or a missing dependant.

For group policies, the system needs to handle additions and deletions in the middle of a policy year, with the effective date of each change recorded. For retail policies, it needs to pick up renewals and portability details from the insurer.

What to check

  • Bulk upload with row-level error reports, so HR teams can fix their own file.
  • Dependant relationships and age rules per policy.
  • E-cards delivered by app, email or SMS, with a way to reissue them.
  • A history of every change to a member record, with who made it and when.

What does hospital network management involve?

It means keeping a current list of empanelled hospitals, the tariffs agreed with each one, and the packages they accept. When a pre-authorization request arrives, the system should know at once whether the hospital is in the network for that insurer and what rate applies.

Tariffs change, hospitals get added or delisted, and different insurers may have different arrangements with the same hospital. The software should store tariffs with start and end dates so an old claim is checked against the rate that applied on the admission date.

How should the cashless pre-authorization desk work?

The desk receives a request from a network hospital, checks eligibility and policy terms, and sends a response back while the patient waits for admission or discharge. Each insurer client will have its own turnaround expectations for cashless requests and discharge approvals, so the software should store those targets per insurer and per request type and measure every case against them.

The processor's time goes into gathering and checking information, so the software should show everything needed in one screen: member details, balance sum insured, sub-limits such as room rent, waiting periods, exclusions and previous claims on the policy.

What to check

  • A queue sorted by time remaining against each insurer's turnaround target, with alerts before it runs out.
  • Rule checks that run as soon as the request lands, so the processor reviews flags instead of typing data.
  • Structured query letters for missing documents, with the clock paused and resumed correctly.
  • Enhancement requests and final bill authorization linked to the original pre-auth.

This desk works against the tightest clock, so it is a sensible first place for claim automation. Our claim automation service covers document reading and policy checks for this desk.

What does reimbursement claim processing need?

Reimbursement claims arrive as a bundle of scanned bills, discharge summaries, prescriptions and reports, often incomplete. The software has to receive them through several channels, extract the data, check it against the policy and produce a recommendation the insurer can act on.

Document extraction is where good and weak systems differ most. Look at how the product handles a handwritten pharmacy bill or a discharge summary from a small nursing home, not only a clean printed invoice. Ask for line-item results: which bill items were allowed, which were deducted, and the policy clause behind each deduction.

How do you manage rules and reports for several insurers?

Keep each insurer's products, rules, letter templates and reports as configuration, separate from the code. A TPA may service many insurers, and each one has its own product wordings, approval limits, escalation matrix and report formats.

If adding a new insurer or a new product means a developer has to change code, every onboarding becomes a software release. A rules screen that trained operations staff can edit, with versioning and an approval step, avoids that. Data for each insurer should be separated so that one insurer's staff can only see their own members and claims.

Reporting deserves the same attention. Insurers ask for claim registers, turnaround reports, outstanding claims and MIS in their own formats and on their own schedules. Scheduled reports that run without manual exports reduce the end-of-month scramble.

What should call centre and grievance handling include?

Agents need a single view of the member: policy, e-card, claims in progress, documents pending and past calls. Without that view, a caller asking about a claim status gets transferred or told to email.

Grievances need their own workflow, with a reference number, an owner, a due date and an escalation path. The record should show what the member was told and when, because the insurer may ask for it when it reviews a complaint.

How should it handle fraud checks, duplicates and audit trails?

The platform should flag suspicious patterns for a person to review, and record every action so any decision can be traced later.

Useful checks include duplicate bills across claims, the same document submitted under two policies, admissions that start soon after policy inception, and hospital billing that sits well above the agreed tariff. Each flag should show its reason, so a reviewer can accept or dismiss it in seconds.

Insurer clients will want to see the history behind any claim they question. In software terms, that means an audit log that cannot be edited, covering data changes, rule overrides, recommendations and who viewed a record. Ask how long entries are kept and how quickly the full trail for one claim can be exported.

Which integrations matter, including NHCX?

The main ones are insurer core systems, hospital systems, NHCX, payment rails and messaging. Each insurer client will have its own way of exchanging policy data, claim recommendations and payment files, so a flexible integration layer matters more than any single connector.

The National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX), a claims exchange run by the National Health Authority, uses FHIR R4 profiles published by NRCeS. The profiles cover coverage eligibility checks, pre-authorization, claims and claim responses. When a vendor says they support NHCX, ask which of these message types they have live and with whom.

Medical coding is the other piece. Diagnosis codes based on WHO's ICD classification make claims comparable across hospitals and insurers, and they make fraud and cost analysis possible.

How should TPA software protect health data?

Treat every claim file as sensitive, because it holds diagnoses, hospital bills, bank details and identity documents together. Restrict access by role and by insurer, give each user only the access their job needs, encrypt data at rest and in transit, and log every view and change.

Ask where the vendor hosts data, who at the vendor can reach production data, and whether member details are masked in test environments. Agree with each insurer client how long closed claim files are kept, and check that the software can delete or anonymise them per insurer.

How do you move data from a legacy TPA system?

Treat migration as its own project with a named owner on the TPA side. A typical list covers active members and dependants, policies and endorsements, hospital tariffs with their effective dates, open pre-authorizations and claims, and closed claim history with scanned documents.

Map every field and record the decisions, such as how an old free-text diagnosis field becomes a coded one. Run trial migrations on a copy of real data and reconcile member counts per policy, claim counts per status and amounts paid per insurer.

Avoid cutting over at month-end, when insurer reports are due. We suggest moving closed history first and open claims last, so each live case sits in one system at a time, and keeping the old system readable for a few months.

Should a TPA buy software or build its own?

Buy if your processes are close to how most TPAs work and you need to go live quickly. Build, or build on top of a bought core, if your process is part of how you win insurer contracts or the products on the market do not fit your volumes and rules.

Buying carries its own risks. Customisation requests can sit in a vendor's queue for months, and leaving a product later means a data migration. Building needs an internal owner who understands claims, and someone has to maintain the system for years.

A middle path is to keep a bought core for policy and member administration, and to build the parts where you differ, such as the pre-authorization desk, document extraction or insurer-specific reporting. Our custom software service covers that kind of build.

What questions should you ask any TPA software vendor?

Ask questions that make the vendor show working software with your own documents and rules. These apply to us as much as to anyone else.

  1. Can you process ten of our real claim files, including a handwritten bill, during the demo?
  2. How do we add a new insurer or product without a code change, and who can approve rule changes?
  3. How does the system track pre-authorization turnaround against each insurer's targets?
  4. Which NHCX message types do you support live today, and with which insurers?
  5. Where is our data hosted, who at your company can access it, and how is that access logged?
  6. What does your audit log record, and can anyone edit or delete entries?
  7. How will you move our data out of our current system, and how will you show that nothing was lost?
  8. What happens to our data and code if we end the contract?
  9. How are you paid: licence, per user, per claim, or a project fee? What raises the cost?
  10. Who fixes problems at 2 a.m. when the cashless desk is down, and how fast?

Frequently asked questions

What is TPA software?

TPA software is the system a Third-Party Administrator uses to service health policies for its insurer clients. It covers member enrolment and e-cards, the hospital network, cashless pre-authorization, reimbursement claims, the call centre and reporting back to each insurer. Most TPAs run it as one platform with separate rule sets per insurer.

How does TPA software handle several insurers at once?

It keeps each insurer's products, rules, letter templates and report formats as configuration rather than code. Operations staff edit rules through a screen with versioning and an approval step, and each insurer's staff see only their own members and claims. A TPA can then onboard a new insurer without a software release.

Does TPA software need to connect to NHCX?

The National Health Claims Exchange (NHCX) is a claims exchange run by the National Health Authority, and it uses FHIR R4 profiles published by NRCeS. When to connect depends on what your insurer clients and network hospitals ask for. New software should be able to send and receive NHCX's FHIR messages even if you start with a few hospitals.

How do you move data from an old TPA system?

List everything first: members, policies, hospital tariffs, open claims and closed claim history with documents. Map each field, run trial migrations, reconcile record counts and amounts, and then run the old and new systems side by side for a short period. Move open pre-authorizations last and avoid month-end reporting days.

How long does it take to build TPA software?

It depends on how many modules you build and how many insurers you onboard first. A single module, such as a pre-authorization desk or a reimbursement intake tool, is a much smaller project than a full platform. We suggest building one module, running it on live claims and then adding the next.
